## Overview

Use eight tasks from your own backlog. If you cannot list eight, use `candidate-backlog.csv`, which has default scores to compare against.

**Score honestly.** A task that sounds exciting but runs twice a year is not a Skill.

**Acceptance criteria:** you can name the three criteria that count *against* a task.

## Part 2: Score and Decide

### Step 3: Score All Six Criteria, 1 to 5

Do not compute the total yet.

**Acceptance criteria:** every task scored on all six criteria.

> **Common Pitfall:** if everything scores a 4 or 5 on impact, you are not discriminating. Force at least two tasks to a 2 or lower.

> **Common Pitfall:** do not flip the three inverted criteria yourself. The formula already flips them. Score each criterion as described.

### Step 4: Compute Each Score

Use the formula in the guide.

**Expected result:** a total for each of the eight tasks.

### Step 5: Apply the Decision Rule

Decide Hold, Skill, or One-off prompt for each task.

**Acceptance criteria:** a decision for all eight.

> **Key Insight:** the Hold rule overrides score. A very sensitive task can rank high and still be a Hold.

## Troubleshooting Reference

| Issue | Symptom | Solution |
|---|---|---|
| No discrimination | Everything scores 4 or 5 on impact | Force at least two tasks to a 2 or lower |
| Criteria double-flipped | Scores look inverted | The guide flips the three inverted criteria in the formula. Score each criterion as described |
| No data owner | A task has nobody accountable for its data | Score sensitivity high and let the decision rule handle it |
| Sensitive task ranks high | A risky task tops the list | The Hold rule overrides score |
| Business case is vague | No numbers in the case | Write a minutes estimate, even a rough one |
